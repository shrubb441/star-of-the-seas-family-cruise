Room views

Full walkthrough tours of both room types, filmed on Star of the Seas itself — so nobody has to guess what they’re walking into.

Rooms 10524 & 10526

Ocean View with Balcony

The standard balcony stateroom, and the right call for both families: private outdoor space, real ocean light, and enough room to spread out without paying suite money.

  • Private ocean-facing balcony with seating
  • Bed positioned toward the balcony, sitting/desk area behind
  • Full bathroom with shower
  • Sofa or convertible bedding for the kids, depending on configuration
  • Wardrobe and under-bed storage for a week of luggage

Exact bedding and sofa setup varies by cabin — confirm the configuration for 10524 and 10526 when you finalise the booking.

Room 10530

Sky Junior Suite

The biggest room of the three. Junior-suite square footage means a genuine sitting area rather than a bed with a chair squeezed next to it, plus a larger balcony and a better bathroom.

  • Noticeably larger than the balcony staterooms
  • Separate sitting area with sofa and table
  • Bathroom with a tub as well as a shower
  • Dressing area and much more hanging storage
  • Wider balcony — room for loungers, not just two chairs
  • Suite-tier service perks on top of the room itself

Sky Junior Suite perks vary by ship and sailing — worth confirming exactly which suite benefits apply to 10530 on this Star of the Seas departure.

Pre-cruise night

Hyatt Place Cape Canaveral

Replaces the old Orlando hotel plan. Staying on the coast instead of inland means a short transfer on embarkation morning rather than an hour-plus drive with nine people and all the luggage.

  • Minutes from the Port Canaveral cruise terminals
  • Paid shuttle to the terminal — book it ahead, not on the day
  • Breakfast on site, so nobody is hunting for food before boarding
  • Rooftop bar with port views if people land early enough

Shuttle seats have to be reserved in advance, so lock that in once flights are booked. Hotel website.

Getting there

Flights and transfers

Fly BDA → MCO on Sat Aug 7, then transfer to Cape Canaveral for the night. Everyone is in one place the evening before, which removes the single biggest thing that goes wrong on cruise mornings.

On Sun Aug 15 the ship is back in Port Canaveral in the morning. Either fly MCO → BDA the same day with a comfortable buffer, or add a second Florida night and fly out relaxed.

Ashore

Ports worth caring about

Keep the ports simple, and make CocoCay the premium day with a real Coco Beach Club cabana target.

Mon Aug 9

CocoCay

Royal’s private island, and the day to build around comfort.

  • Coco Beach Club cabana or floating cabana
  • Beach Club infinity pool and upgraded lunch
  • Thrill Waterpark for kids and teens
  • Oasis Lagoon, Splashaway Bay, Chill Island
Wed Aug 11

Costa Maya

Lowest-stress port for mixed ages.

  • Port pool and shopping
  • Maya Chan or a beach club day
  • Chacchoben ruins
  • Keep it flexible if people are tired
Thu Aug 12

Roatán

The nature and adventure stop.

  • West Bay beach
  • Reef snorkelling
  • Sloths, monkeys and island tour
  • Zipline or private driver day
Fri Aug 13

Cozumel

Best for an easy beach club or water day.

  • Nachi Cocom or Mr. Sanchos style beach club
  • Snorkel or catamaran
  • Jeep or island tour
  • Ruins only if people want a longer day

Cabana call

Best target: a Coco Beach Club cabana. Dream target: a floating cabana.

Why: adults get a nicer base, kids still get island fun, and CocoCay becomes the premium anchor of the whole trip.

Coco Beach Club cabanas usually include Beach Club access for cabana guests, attendant service, an upgraded lunch, infinity pool access and a quieter beach setup.
